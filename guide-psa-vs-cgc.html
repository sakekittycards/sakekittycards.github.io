PSA vs CGC vs BGS for Pokémon Cards

They all put your card in a plastic case with a number on it. What differs is how easily you can sell that case afterwards — and for Pokémon specifically, that difference is not small.

The short version

Comparison of PSA, CGC and BGS for Pokemon card grading
PSACGCBGS
Resale liquidityDeepest for PokémonStrong and growingThinner on modern Pokémon
SubgradesNoOn requestYes, always — the main draw
Top gradePSA 10 Gem MintCGC 10 Pristine / Perfect 10BGS 10 Pristine, Black Label 10
Best forAnything you intend to sellModern bulk, value submissionsCards you believe are flawless
Watch out forCost and wait at busy timesSlab sells below the PSA equivalentA 9.5 is common; the premium is in the 10

Fees and turnaround times change frequently at all three companies. Check their current published pricing before you build a submission around any of this.

PSA

PSA is the default for Pokémon and the reason is market history, not grading philosophy. More Pokémon cards have been graded by PSA than by anyone else, which means when you go to sell a PSA slab there is a long record of comparable sales at that exact grade. Pricing it is easy, and so is selling it.

That liquidity is a real, measurable premium. On the same card at the same nominal grade, a PSA slab typically clears higher than the alternatives. If your intention is to sell the card, that premium is usually worth more than any fee difference elsewhere.

The trade-off is that PSA has no subgrades — you get one number and no explanation. A card that just missed a 10 and a card that comfortably made a 9 look identical in the slab.

CGC

CGC came to Pokémon from comics and built a serious reputation quickly. The slab is excellent, the presentation is clean, and on modern submissions it is frequently better value per card, which matters a lot when you are submitting a stack rather than a single chase card.

CGC also offers subgrades on request, so you can get the diagnostic detail without going all the way to BGS. Historically CGC has been regarded as tougher on centering and surface for modern cards, which is a point in favor of the grade meaning something — but do not assume a card that would be a PSA 10 walks into a CGC 10.

The honest caveat is resale. On the same card, a CGC 10 generally does not clear what a PSA 10 does. That gap is closing, but it is real today, and it should be part of the math before you submit rather than a surprise afterwards.

BGS

BGS is the subgrade company. Every slab carries separate marks for centering, corners, edges and surface, and the overall grade derives from those four. For anyone who actually wants to understand their card — or to prove its quality to a buyer — that transparency is genuinely valuable.

The famous prize is the Black Label 10: a perfect 10 in all four subgrades. It is rare enough that it commands an enormous premium over an ordinary BGS 10, which is exactly why people chase it. The corollary is that a BGS 9.5 is comparatively common, and the price gap between a 9.5 and a 10 is where all the money lives.

For modern Pokémon specifically, BGS resale is thinner than PSA. It is the right choice when you genuinely believe the card is flawless and you want the subgrades to prove it. It is the wrong choice as a default.

So which one?

  • You intend to sell it: PSA. The liquidity premium is real and it usually outweighs everything else.
  • You are submitting a stack of modern cards: CGC is often the better economics, especially on cards where the difference between a 9 and a 10 is not life-changing.
  • You are convinced the card is perfect: BGS, and chase the Black Label. Just be honest with yourself about “convinced.”
  • You are keeping it forever: whichever slab you like looking at. This is a legitimate answer and nobody should talk you out of it.
  • You have no idea if the card will grade well: do not submit it yet. Screen it first — that is a $3 to $5 question, not a $30 one.

Grading company questions

Which grading company is best for Pokemon cards? +
For resale liquidity, PSA. It has the deepest sold-comp history for Pokemon, which means a PSA slab is the easiest to price and the easiest to sell. CGC is a strong second and is often better value on modern bulk submissions. BGS is worth it mainly for the subgrades and for a Black Label chase.
Does a CGC 10 sell for as much as a PSA 10? +
Generally no, on the same card. The gap varies by era and by card, but PSA slabs command a premium that reflects market familiarity more than grading accuracy. Factor that into the decision before you submit, not after.
What is the difference between a BGS 9.5 and a BGS 10? +
BGS grades four subgrades — centering, corners, edges and surface — and the overall grade is derived from them. A BGS 10 Pristine requires near-perfection across all four; the Black Label 10 requires a perfect 10 in every subgrade and is genuinely rare, which is why it carries such a premium.
Do the graders use the same scale? +
All three use a 1-to-10 scale, but they are not interchangeable. Historically CGC has been considered stricter on centering and surface for modern cards. Do not assume a card that would be a PSA 10 is automatically a CGC 10 or vice versa.
